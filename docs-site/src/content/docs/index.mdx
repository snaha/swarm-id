Swarm ID is sign-in and storage for apps built on [Swarm](https://www.ethswarm.org/). Your users
get one account that works in every Swarm app, unlocked with a passkey, a password or an Ethereum
wallet, in the browser they already have. Your app gets a signed-in user and somewhere to put their
data, with no servers, no session store and nothing to install.

## What your users see

Your app shows a connect button. From there:

The account is a recovery phrase. The user writes it down once, when the account is created, and
can restore the account from it on any device. Day to day they unlock with the passkey, wallet or
password they chose instead. Creating an account, signing in and downloading can be done without
payment. Uploading needs storage, which is paid for, below.

Try it on [the demo app](https://swarm-demo.snaha.net). Press **Connect** in the sidebar and you
have an account two minutes from now. A new account has no storage of its own yet, so turn on
**Use subsidised gateway** in the sidebar (or add a drive, below). The demo then lets you upload a
file, publish a feed and share something with one chosen reader, which is roughly what any app on
Swarm does.

## One account, every app, every device

Swarm ID is also where people manage that account. The identity site lists the apps they have
connected, lets them disconnect one, and holds their **drives**: prepaid storage on Swarm that any
connected app can write to.

A drive is where payment enters. It follows the account: sign in on a second laptop and the same
drives are there; upload from either and it lands in the same place. Storage belongs to the user
and is paid for by the user, in crypto, through a payment widget on the identity site or from an
Ethereum account they connect for it. A drive is bought for a period and has to be extended before
it runs out. Your app never holds a payment method or a storage budget for anyone.

Every new account starts without a drive, so it can read but not write. Until the user adds one,
your app either sends them to the identity site's Storage tab or picks up the bill itself through
a [subsidised gateway](./subsidised-gateway/). Downloads are free either way.

## What your app gets

- **Sign-in in one call.** The library opens the popup, waits for the user and hands you the
  connection.
- **Storage through the same connection.** Upload, download, publish a feed, encrypt for a chosen
  set of readers. Every Swarm request goes out on the user's behalf; uploads are paid from the
  user's drive, or from a gateway you subsidise.
- **Keys you never hold.** The secret that controls the account never leaves the Swarm ID site, and
  the key derived from it for your app stays in the iframe: your app gets signatures and derived
  values, never the key itself. One app's key says nothing about another's, and nothing your app
  holds can expose the account. Isolation is of keys, not of data: anything uploaded unencrypted
  is public on Swarm, as it is for every Swarm app.
- **Every major browser.** Chrome, Firefox and Safari, desktop and mobile, no extension. This is the
  part that is usually hard on the web, and it is checked on real devices;
  [Architecture](./architecture/#storage) says what is confirmed and what is still open.

The identity site at [swarm-id.snaha.net](https://swarm-id.snaha.net) is hosted for you, so an
integration is a package install and a few lines. Everything is open source, and you can
[host your own](./self-hosting/) when you want the sign-in page on your domain. The account is the
recovery phrase, so it is the same identity on any host; what a user sets up again on yours is
[how they unlock it](./self-hosting/#what-it-costs).
