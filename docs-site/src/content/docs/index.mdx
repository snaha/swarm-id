Swarm ID gives the people who use your app one account that works across every app built on
[Swarm](https://www.ethswarm.org/). They sign in with a passkey, a password or an Ethereum
wallet, in whatever browser they already have, with nothing to install. Your app gets a signed-in
user and a place to store their data on Swarm, without running any of the infrastructure that
usually comes with either.

It is for anyone building on Swarm: a developer who wants sign-in and storage done in an
afternoon, or a product owner deciding how users will get into the app at all. This page shows
what your users see, what your app gets, and where to go next.

The account is a normal recovery phrase under the hood, so it can be restored on any device, but
nobody has to see a phrase to get started. A user who picks a password or a passkey never touches
a wallet.

Try it yourself at [the demo app](https://swarm-demo.snaha.net): press **Connect** in the sidebar,
and you have an account two minutes from now. The demo then lets you upload a file, publish a
feed and share something with a chosen reader, which is roughly what any app on Swarm does.

## One account, every app, every device

Swarm ID is also where your users manage that account. The identity site lists the apps they have
connected and lets them disconnect one, and holds their **drives**: prepaid storage on Swarm that
any connected app can write to.

Drives are bought once and follow the account. A user who signs in on a second laptop finds the
same drives there, and uploads from either device land in the same place. For you this means
storage is the user's, paid for by them, and your app never has to hold a payment method or a
storage budget on anyone's behalf. If you would rather sponsor it, a
[subsidised gateway](./subsidised-gateway/) covers the storage for them.

## What your app gets

- **Sign-in in one call.** The library opens the popup, waits for the user, and hands you the
  connection. There is no server to run and no session store to keep.
- **Storage through the same connection.** Upload, download, publish a feed, encrypt for a
  chosen set of readers. The library makes every Swarm request on the user's behalf, using the
  user's drive.
- **Keys you do not have to keep.** Each app receives its own key, derived for that app alone.
  Two apps connected to one account cannot read each other's data, and losing your app's key
  cannot expose the account. The secret that controls the account never leaves the Swarm ID
  site.
- **Every major browser.** Chrome, Firefox and Safari, desktop and mobile, with no extension. This
  is the part that is usually hard on the web, and it is verified on real devices.

The identity site at [swarm-id.snaha.net](https://swarm-id.snaha.net) is hosted for you, so an
integration is a package install and a few lines. The code is open source, and you can
[host your own](./self-hosting/) copy when you want the sign-in page on your domain.
